# Trimming T4 hours without touching quality

**Realistically, 10-25% of the GPU-hours can be removed with parity-checked, quality-neutral changes, and up to roughly 35% only if two conditional bets pay off. Nothing in the research reaches a 2x cut.** The ceiling is set by what is already done. The diffusion U-Net already trains in fp16 with channels_last and fused EMA, so the cheap tensor-core win is spent, and the published evidence for the remaining kernel-level tricks on a T4 is small: torch.compile adds roughly 3-15% in T4 inference tables and sometimes nothing ([Diffusers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/optimization/torch2.0), [PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/no-speedup-from-torch-complile/175497)). The two largest possible savings are conditional. The first is fp16 mixed precision for the fp32 codec, where an estimated 1.2-1.8x on the codec's share (about 8-40 GPU-hours) is an inference with no published rate-distortion evidence in either direction. The second is repairing the input pipeline, which pays off only if the 41 GB memory-mapped cache is thrashing on a ~29 GB RAM machine, a failure mode that cut one documented loader from about 60 to about 3 batches/s ([PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/numpy-memmap-throttles-with-dataloader-when-available-ram-less-than-file-size/83274)). Evaluation (20-30 T4-hours) offers exact savings from reusing the shared prefix of guided and unguided runs, fp16 sampling, and caching per-image invariants, together perhaps 3-10 hours. **No T4 benchmark exists for this model family in any of the research notes**, so every speedup figure below that is not a quoted external number is an inference and must be measured in a 10-minute micro-benchmark before it is trusted. The rest of the savings is wall-clock, not GPU-hours: renting a 4090 or A100 for the diffusion critical path can cut calendar time several-fold for roughly $6-27 (unverified speedups), but it does not reduce T4-hours.

## What is already banked and what the evidence can and cannot show

Four of the five obvious levers are already implemented (decoded-image cache, cudnn.benchmark, channels_last for the U-Net, fused EMA, and a two-GPU job graph), so the question is what remains. The research gives an unusually clear answer about its own quality: external evidence is thin and mostly inference-only. The diffusion notes found no T4 training benchmark for a diffusers UNet2DModel, no graph-break report for it, and no source on GroupNorm with channels_last behaviour. The codec notes found no paper reporting AMP training of a Ballé/Minnen/Cheng-style codec with rate-distortion numbers, and no CompressAI discussion of fp16 GDN. The evaluation notes found no T4 numbers for a pixel-space 768x512 U-Net. Hardware notes could not retrieve official Kaggle documentation, so quota and disk facts are secondary. The honest reading is that the strongest claims in this report are structural arguments (what is exact, what is not) rather than measured speedups. Where an external figure exists it comes from different hardware or workloads: the Databricks Stable Diffusion 2 cost reduction (about 7.45 to 6.79 days on A100s) is a different scale ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/stable-diffusion-2)), and the FFCV result that per-sample decode and crop, not raw disk reading, dominated a PyTorch loader used 96 vCPUs, not 4 ([FFCV](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12517v1)).

## Evidence behind the top-ranked items

**Input pipeline.** The notes carry a specific warning that applies directly to this project: a 41 GB memory-mapped cache on a roughly 29 GB RAM machine does not fit in the page cache, so a fraction of random-crop reads hit disk. In the documented case a float32 memmap larger than RAM with shuffled access fell from about 60 batches/s to about 3 with about 500 MB/s of constant disk reads against about 8 MB/s needed, with no confirmed fix ([PyTorch forum](https://discuss.pytorch.org/t/numpy-memmap-throttles-with-dataloader-when-available-ram-less-than-file-size/83274)). The geometry makes it worse here. With an (H, W, 3) layout, a 128-pixel crop is 128 separate 384-byte segments at a 6120-byte stride, each pulling at least a 4 KB page, so about 0.5 MB of page-granular reads per sample (arithmetic from the notes). Whether Kaggle's disk can serve about 200-400 samples/s of such scattered reads is unmeasured, and so is whether the container memory limit counts page cache. Two trainers share one page cache for one file, so a single shared file beats two copies. Mitigations that preserve the sampling distribution are a tiled layout so a crop touches 1-4 contiguous chunks, MADV_RANDOM to stop useless readahead, and possibly lz4/zstd tiles to bring 41 GB under about 29 GB if photos compress 1.5-2x (a general-knowledge estimate; benchmark it). Caching only a subset in RAM or on GPU, or using lossy or GPU-JPEG decoding, would bias the sampling distribution or break exact pixels and is excluded; nvImageCodec lists no GPU PNG path ([NVIDIA nvImageCodec](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/nvimagecodec/index.html)). This item is ranked first because it is a diagnosis that costs minutes: time the wait in `next(loader)` against step time, and feed one fixed cached batch repeatedly. If it/s does not jump, the loader is innocent and the saving is zero.

**Codec mixed precision.** The codec is fp32 on a T4, which delivers roughly 8 TFLOPS fp32 peak against much higher fp16 tensor-core throughput, so this is the single largest remaining compute lever. A 2026 benchmark paper reports T4 FP16 at about 1.8-2.2x over FP32 on classification CNNs ([arXiv 2604.14552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14552)), but the codec's GDN, entropy model and noise or rounding are memory-bound fp32 work AMP cannot accelerate, which is why the notes estimate 1.2-1.8x end to end. The notes derive the precision plan from library source and PyTorch AMP docs. CompressAI's GDN is a 1x1 conv on x**2 followed by rsqrt with no dtype handling, and x**2 overflows fp16 above |x| of 256 ([CompressAI gdn.py](https://github.com/InterDigitalInc/CompressAI/blob/master/compressai/layers/gdn.py)). The likelihood floor of 1e-9 is below fp16's smallest subnormal (about 6e-8), so in fp16 it silently becomes zero and produces log(0) and NaN bpp. `erfc`, which the Gaussian CDF uses, is on neither autocast list, so it runs in whatever dtype it receives ([PyTorch AMP docs](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/amp.html)). The recommended recipe is therefore fp16 only for the plain strided convs, with GDN, entropy model, likelihood, loss and the auxiliary loss in fp32, plus a GradScaler whose skipped steps are logged, since skipped steps slightly alter the effective iteration count. Treat this as an experiment. Because T4 has no bf16 tensor cores, fp16 is the only option, and TF32 (a rental-GPU alternative) also changes numerics. Gradient accumulation or larger batch with scaled LR is not equivalent and gives no speedup on a compute-bound T4, so it is out ([Goyal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02677) validates linear scaling only for SGD on classification).

**Evaluation.** Because decoded images are independent, the evaluation split across two processes pinned to separate GPUs is exact and near-2x, and is already in the job graph; DataParallel would do nothing at batch 1. The new exact savings are reuse. For unguided and guided runs sharing a seed and a deterministic sampler (eta = 0), the trajectories are identical until guidance starts, so the shared prefix can be computed once. The notes put this at 30-50% of a paired run, but if guidance starts at t<=700 on a uniform schedule the shared prefix is about 30% of the steps of one run, roughly 15% of the pair; I use the lower figure. At the first step the cond and uncond predictions are identical across all five CFG scales, saving about 2-4% of steps. No literature on CFG-sweep trajectory sharing exists; this is the notes' own reasoning and needs a bitwise check at the branch step. Per-image caches (decoded latents per image and bitrate, original-image OCR, FaceNet embeddings, LPIPS features, clean-fid reference statistics built once via `fid.make_custom_stats` ([clean-fid](https://github.com/GaParmar/clean-fid))) and decode-then-metrics staging are exact. On fp16 sampling, SDPA gives 20-28% over standard attention in fp16 across the PyTorch blog's tested GPUs and compile adds 13-22% on most workloads there ([PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerated-diffusers-pt-20/)), but the same post notes a T4 regression at batch 16, and none of it is for a 24,576-token pixel-space U-Net. Check that attention actually dispatches to SDPA's memory-efficient backend, since flash does not run on sm75. The riskiest change is anything touching the bin-consistency guidance through g_a: run the three gradient steps under autocast if parity allows, but compute the reported consistency rate and final bin decisions in fp32, because values within about 0.01 of a 0.5-bin boundary can flip.

## Using the second T4 and extra capacity without gaming providers

DDP is not the right tool for the second GPU. The only Kaggle T4 x2 measurement found, for a 4M-parameter transformer, showed DDP slower than one GPU (26.36 vs 22.03 ms/step, 41.8% scaling efficiency) ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/thokozani_buthelezi_2cd41/ddp-is-not-always-faster-23mh)). A 35M-parameter U-Net moves about 140 MB of fp32 gradients per step over PCIe without NVLink, which could rival a batch-16 step at 128 pixels, and disabling fast interconnects slowed 2-GPU DDP from 101 to 131 s in Hugging Face's tests ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/transformers/v4.8.0/performance.html)). Independent jobs, one per GPU via CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES, scale perfectly and keep results identical, which is what the existing job graph does ([Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/a-journal-asked-for-three-seeds-we-had-a-laptop-and-euro0)). Whether a T4 x2 session is charged as one or two quota hours is unverified in any source; watch the quota counter for 10-15 minutes. Quota is commonly reported as about 30 GPU-hours a week with 12-hour sessions, and the notes disagree on session length (9 h in one vendor source) ([gpuperhour](https://gpuperhour.com/blog/free-cloud-gpus-and-credits)). Kaggle's output area is about 20 GB, so the 41 GB cache cannot be saved there; rebuilding it each session from the attached PNG dataset (about 1-2 minutes of decode by the notes' arithmetic, plus write time) is likely cheaper than restoring it. Legitimate extra capacity comes from combining different services, not from extra accounts at one provider: do not run multiple accounts at one provider, which violates provider terms. Options are Colab pay-as-you-go at about $0.12 per T4-hour (about $24 for a full 200 T4-hours, a second independent stream with no speedup) ([McCormick](https://mccormickml.com/2024/04/23/colab-gpus-features-and-pricing/)), the reported but possibly ending Colab Pro link bonus of +15 or +30 Kaggle hours a week, and short rentals. RunPod lists an RTX 4090 at $0.34-0.89 an hour and an A100 80GB at $1.19-2.09 depending on tier ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)); Vast.ai medians were about $0.50 and $0.84 on 16 November 2026 ([Markaicode](https://markaicode.com/pricing/vast-ai-pricing/)), with marketplace volatility. If the assumed 4-5x fp16 speedup over a T4 holds (unbenchmarked, and 30-50% lower if the loader or the rental's CPU is the bottleneck), the roughly 65 T4-hour diffusion job takes about 13-16 hours for about $6-27, and the critical path of about 90 T4-hours (20 base, 6 fine-tune, 65 diffusion) falls to about 22 hours. This cuts wall-clock, not T4 GPU-hours. Lightning AI is poor value now (T4 reportedly $0.55/h after a September 2026 repricing), and free tiers such as ZeroGPU and Paperspace are unsuitable ([UsagePricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/lightning-ai-2026-09-21-gpu-rate-card-reprice)). Keep the codec in fp32 on rental hardware only if exact numerics matter, since TF32 is on by default for cuDNN convs on Ampere and later.

## What is not worth doing, and how to verify what is

Several popular ideas fail the "identical" constraint or the cost-benefit test. Min-SNR weighting, adaptive timestep sampling, SpeeD, v-prediction and EDM-style changes alter the objective, so they are not parity-preserving; the best-evidenced, Min-SNR, shows a modest eps-prediction gain on a large ADM-style U-Net that shrinks with training (FID 8.55 to 7.32 at 200K iterations, 4.21 to 4.14 at 1M) ([arXiv 2303.09556](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09556v3)), with no test on small conditional pixel-space codec-refinement U-Nets. Larger batch or accumulation changes the optimisation. Gradient checkpointing trades compute for memory and only slows training if batch 32 at 128 pixels already fits. CUDA graphs and `reduce-overhead` need static shapes and help only if the GPU idles on Python launch overhead, which the data-bound test should establish first. xFormers adds nothing over SDPA, 1DN-style GDN replacements change the model, GPU image decoding is lossy or unsupported for PNG, and subset caches bias sampling. TRC and torchao are not helpful here (TRC needs a TPU port; T4 lacks fp8 and bf16). Post-hoc EMA is safe but saves no compute. For verification, run a 50-warmup, 200-step micro-benchmark for every toggle singly and combined, with `torch.cuda.synchronize()` and loader time excluded; for numerically equivalent changes compare step-0 loss and gradient norm and then loss curves within a band measured from three baseline seeds; for the codec, add gradient cosine above about 0.99, bpp error under about 0.1-0.5%, zero non-finite likelihoods, a short three-seed A/B at a low and a high lambda, a half-length (75k-iteration) run, and a final check that actual bitstream size still matches estimated bpp. For evaluation, same-seed paired deltas with bootstrap CIs against seed-to-seed noise, McNemar-style flip counts for TFR, FIR and consistency rate, and a repeat-run check that exposes non-determinism from cudnn.benchmark. The tolerance thresholds are the project's choice; none are published, and 20 images has little power for rare flips.

## Conclusion

The practical sequence is measure, then diagnose, then spend effort. Spend 30 minutes on the micro-benchmark and data-bound test, because they decide whether the biggest possible saving is a loader fix (zero or large) and whether the codec's share justifies an AMP experiment. Attempt the codec fp16 hybrid only with the fp32 islands and the multi-seed A/B protocol, and treat a failed parity test as a reason to keep fp32 rather than to loosen tolerances. Do the exact evaluation changes next, since they carry the least risk, then use cheap rental capacity for wall-clock if the schedule matters more than T4-hours. A realistic outcome is 120-200 GPU-hours becoming about 95-165, and 65-120 wall-clock hours becoming perhaps 50-100 on free quota, or far less with a few dollars of 4090 time on the critical path (all estimates, unmeasured).

The less obvious conclusion is that the notes' most valuable output is a ranking of uncertainty, not of speed. Everything exact (prefix reuse, caching, independent two-GPU jobs, layout-only loader changes) is cheap and certain but small; everything large (codec AMP, loader repair, rental speedups) is conditional on measurements nobody has made. The first 10 minutes of benchmarking will tell the project more about where its hours go than any further literature.
